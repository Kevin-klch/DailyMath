A mixed number combines a whole number and a proper fraction, like $2\frac{3}{4}$ – "two and three quarters". Mixed numbers are how we naturally talk about amounts in everyday life: one and a half hours, two and a quarter cups of flour. For calculating, however, it is often easier to switch to improper fractions, so you need to move confidently between the two forms.

## What a mixed number means

The mixed number $2\frac{3}{4}$ is short for a **sum**:

$$2\frac{3}{4} = 2 + \frac{3}{4}$$

It does *not* mean $2 \cdot \frac{3}{4}$, even though the two parts are written next to each other. This is the one place in mathematics where writing things side by side means addition.

## Mixed number to improper fraction

Write the whole part as a fraction with the same denominator and add the numerators. As a shortcut: multiply the whole number by the denominator and add the numerator.

$$2\frac{3}{4} = \frac{2 \cdot 4 + 3}{4} = \frac{11}{4}$$

The idea: $2$ wholes are $8$ quarters, plus $3$ more quarters makes $11$ quarters.

> **Key idea:** $a\frac{b}{c} = \frac{a \cdot c + b}{c}$. The denominator stays the same; the new numerator is "whole number times denominator plus numerator".

## Improper fraction to mixed number

Divide the numerator by the denominator. The quotient is the whole part, the remainder becomes the new numerator:

$$\frac{17}{5}: \quad 17 : 5 = 3 \text{ remainder } 2 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \frac{17}{5} = 3\frac{2}{5}$$

Always check that the fractional part is proper (numerator smaller than denominator) and fully simplified.

## Adding mixed numbers

You can add the whole parts and the fractional parts separately, because a mixed number is a sum.

### Example 1

$$2\frac{1}{3} + 1\frac{1}{2}$$

Whole parts: $2 + 1 = 3$. Fractions: $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{6} + \frac{3}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$. Result: $3\frac{5}{6}$.

### Example 2: carrying

$$1\frac{3}{4} + 2\frac{2}{3}$$

Whole parts: $3$. Fractions: $\frac{9}{12} + \frac{8}{12} = \frac{17}{12} = 1\frac{5}{12}$. The extra whole is carried over:

$$3 + 1\frac{5}{12} = 4\frac{5}{12}$$

## Subtracting mixed numbers

Subtract whole parts and fractional parts separately. If the fraction you subtract is larger, **borrow** one whole first.

### Example 3: borrowing

$$4\frac{1}{5} - 1\frac{3}{5}$$

Since $\frac{1}{5} < \frac{3}{5}$, turn one of the $4$ wholes into fifths: $4\frac{1}{5} = 3\frac{6}{5}$. Now

$$3\frac{6}{5} - 1\frac{3}{5} = 2\frac{3}{5}$$

Alternatively, convert both to improper fractions: $\frac{21}{5} - \frac{8}{5} = \frac{13}{5} = 2\frac{3}{5}$. If borrowing feels confusing, this route always works.

## Multiplying and dividing

For multiplication and division you **must** convert to improper fractions first. Multiplying whole parts and fractional parts separately gives wrong answers.

> **Key idea:** Before multiplying or dividing, turn every mixed number into an improper fraction. At the end, convert back if a mixed number is wanted.

### Example 4

$$1\frac{1}{2} \cdot 2\frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{8}{3} = \frac{24}{6} = 4$$

The tempting shortcut $1 \cdot 2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{3} = 2\frac{1}{3}$ is wrong – it ignores the cross terms, just like $(1 + \frac{1}{2})(2 + \frac{2}{3})$ has four products when you expand it.

### Example 5

$$3\frac{3}{4} : 1\frac{1}{4} = \frac{15}{4} : \frac{5}{4} = \frac{15}{4} \cdot \frac{4}{5} = 3$$

## Negative mixed numbers

A minus sign in front applies to the whole mixed number: $-2\frac{1}{2} = -\left(2 + \frac{1}{2}\right) = -\frac{5}{2}$. It is not $-2 + \frac{1}{2}$.

## Common mistakes

- Reading $2\frac{3}{4}$ as $2 \cdot \frac{3}{4}$.
- Converting with the wrong formula, for example $2\frac{3}{4} = \frac{2 + 3}{4}$. Correct is $\frac{11}{4}$.
- Multiplying whole parts and fractions separately.
- Forgetting to carry when the fractional parts add up to more than $1$.
- Subtracting "the smaller from the larger" fraction instead of borrowing.

## Practice

1. Write $3\frac{2}{7}$ as an improper fraction.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$\frac{3 \cdot 7 + 2}{7} = \frac{23}{7}$.

</details>

2. Write $\frac{29}{6}$ as a mixed number.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$29 : 6 = 4$ remainder $5$, so $\frac{29}{6} = 4\frac{5}{6}$.

</details>

3. Calculate $2\frac{5}{6} + 1\frac{3}{4}$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Whole parts: $3$. Fractions: $\frac{10}{12} + \frac{9}{12} = \frac{19}{12} = 1\frac{7}{12}$. Result: $4\frac{7}{12}$.

</details>

4. Calculate $5\frac{1}{4} - 2\frac{2}{3}$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Common denominator: $5\frac{3}{12} - 2\frac{8}{12}$. Borrow: $4\frac{15}{12} - 2\frac{8}{12} = 2\frac{7}{12}$.

</details>

5. Calculate $2\frac{1}{4} \cdot 1\frac{1}{3}$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$\frac{9}{4} \cdot \frac{4}{3} = \frac{36}{12} = 3$.

</details>
