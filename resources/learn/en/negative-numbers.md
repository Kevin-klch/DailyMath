Temperatures below zero, a bank account in the red, a position below sea level – all of these need numbers smaller than zero. **Negative numbers** extend the number line to the left of zero. The rules for calculating with them are few, but they are used in almost every later topic, so it pays to know them by heart.

## The number line

On the number line, positive numbers lie to the right of $0$ and negative numbers to the left. Every positive number has a mirror image on the other side: $3$ and $-3$ are **opposites**. They are the same distance from zero.

The further left a number is, the smaller it is. So

$$-7 < -2 < 0 < 3$$

This feels strange at first: $-7$ is smaller than $-2$, even though $7$ is bigger than $2$. Think of temperatures: $-7$ °C is colder than $-2$ °C.

The distance of a number from zero is its **absolute value**, written $|-7| = 7$ and $|3| = 3$.

## Adding and subtracting

### Adding

Adding a positive number means moving **right** on the number line, adding a negative number means moving **left**.

- $-3 + 5 = 2$ (start at $-3$, move 5 steps right)
- $4 + (-6) = 4 - 6 = -2$ (start at $4$, move 6 steps left)
- $-2 + (-5) = -7$ (two "debts" add up)

A quick rule: if the signs are the same, add the absolute values and keep the sign. If the signs differ, subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger and take the sign of the number with the larger absolute value.

### Subtracting

Subtracting a number is the same as adding its opposite:

$$a - b = a + (-b)$$

That is why subtracting a negative number makes things *bigger*:

$$5 - (-3) = 5 + 3 = 8$$

If someone takes away a debt of 3 €, you are 3 € better off.

> **Key idea:** Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding a positive one: $a - (-b) = a + b$.

## Simplifying signs

When two signs meet, you can merge them:

| Signs | Result | Example |
|---|---|---|
| $+(+)$ | $+$ | $7 + (+2) = 9$ |
| $+(-)$ | $-$ | $7 + (-2) = 5$ |
| $-(+)$ | $-$ | $7 - (+2) = 5$ |
| $-(-)$ | $+$ | $7 - (-2) = 9$ |

Equal signs give plus, different signs give minus.

## Multiplying and dividing

For multiplication and division, work out the result with the absolute values and then decide the sign:

- $(+) \cdot (+) = (+)$, e.g. $3 \cdot 4 = 12$
- $(+) \cdot (-) = (-)$, e.g. $3 \cdot (-4) = -12$
- $(-) \cdot (+) = (-)$, e.g. $(-3) \cdot 4 = -12$
- $(-) \cdot (-) = (+)$, e.g. $(-3) \cdot (-4) = 12$

Division follows exactly the same sign rules: $(-20) : 5 = -4$ and $(-20) : (-5) = 4$.

> **Key idea:** Same signs give a positive result, different signs give a negative result. With several factors, count the minus signs: an even number gives $+$, an odd number gives $-$.

### Why is minus times minus plus?

Look at this pattern and keep going:

$$3 \cdot (-2) = -6,\quad 2 \cdot (-2) = -4,\quad 1 \cdot (-2) = -2,\quad 0 \cdot (-2) = 0$$

Each time the first factor drops by 1, the result goes up by 2. Continuing the pattern gives $(-1) \cdot (-2) = 2$ and $(-2) \cdot (-2) = 4$. Any other rule would break the familiar laws of arithmetic, such as the distributive law.

## Powers of negative numbers

A power is repeated multiplication, so the counting rule applies: an even exponent gives a positive result, an odd exponent keeps the minus.

$$(-2)^3 = (-2) \cdot (-2) \cdot (-2) = -8 \qquad (-2)^4 = 16$$

Watch the parentheses: $-2^4 = -16$, because the power only applies to the $2$.

## Worked examples

### Example 1

$$-8 + 3 - (-6) - 4$$

Turn the double sign into a plus: $-8 + 3 + 6 - 4$. Now work left to right: $-5 + 6 = 1$, then $1 - 4 = -3$.

### Example 2

$$(-3) \cdot (-2) \cdot (-5)$$

Absolute values: $3 \cdot 2 \cdot 5 = 30$. There are three minus signs (odd), so the result is $-30$.

### Example 3

$$-12 : (-4) + 2 \cdot (-3)$$

Multiplication and division first: $-12 : (-4) = 3$ and $2 \cdot (-3) = -6$. Then $3 + (-6) = -3$.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking $-7 > -2$ because $7 > 2$. On the number line, $-7$ lies further left, so it is smaller.
- Applying "minus times minus is plus" to addition: $-3 + (-4)$ is $-7$, not $7$.
- Forgetting to flip the second sign when subtracting: $5 - (-3) = 8$, not $2$.
- Confusing $-3^2 = -9$ with $(-3)^2 = 9$.
- Losing track of the sign in long products – count the minus signs.

## Practice

1. Order from smallest to largest: $-4$, $2$, $-9$, $0$, $-1$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$-9 < -4 < -1 < 0 < 2$.

</details>

2. Calculate $-6 - (-10) + (-3)$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$-6 + 10 - 3 = 4 - 3 = 1$.

</details>

3. Calculate $(-4) \cdot 5 \cdot (-2) \cdot (-1)$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Absolute values: $4 \cdot 5 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 40$. Three minus signs, so the result is $-40$.

</details>

4. Calculate $(-36) : (-9) - 7 \cdot (-2)$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$(-36) : (-9) = 4$ and $7 \cdot (-2) = -14$. Then $4 - (-14) = 4 + 14 = 18$.

</details>

5. The temperature at 6 a.m. is $-5$ °C. By noon it has risen by 12 degrees, and by midnight it has dropped by 15 degrees from the noon value. What is the temperature at midnight?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Noon: $-5 + 12 = 7$ °C. Midnight: $7 - 15 = -8$ °C.

</details>
