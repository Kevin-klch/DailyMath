An equation states that two expressions are equal. A **linear equation** is one in which the variable appears only to the first power – no $x^2$, no $\sqrt{x}$, no $x$ in a denominator. Solving it means finding the value of the variable that makes the statement true. Linear equations are the backbone of algebra: almost every word problem, formula rearrangement and many harder equations eventually come down to one.

## The balance idea

Think of an equation as a balance scale. The left side and the right side weigh the same. If you do the same thing to both sides, the scale stays balanced. These are the allowed **equivalence transformations**:

- add the same number or term to both sides,
- subtract the same number or term from both sides,
- multiply both sides by the same number (not zero),
- divide both sides by the same number (not zero).

> **Key idea:** Whatever you do to one side of an equation, you must do to the other side as well. The goal is to get the variable alone on one side: $x = \ldots$

## The standard procedure

1. **Simplify** each side: expand brackets, combine like terms.
2. **Collect** all terms with the variable on one side and all numbers on the other side (by adding or subtracting).
3. **Divide** by the coefficient of the variable.
4. **Check** your answer by substituting it into the original equation.

### Example 1

$$3x + 5 = 20$$

Subtract $5$ on both sides: $3x = 15$. Divide by $3$: $x = 5$.

Check: $3 \cdot 5 + 5 = 20$. Correct.

### Example 2: variable on both sides

$$5x - 7 = 2x + 11$$

Subtract $2x$: $3x - 7 = 11$. Add $7$: $3x = 18$. Divide by $3$: $x = 6$.

Check: left $5 \cdot 6 - 7 = 23$, right $2 \cdot 6 + 11 = 23$. Correct.

### Example 3: brackets

$$4(x - 2) = 2(x + 3)$$

Expand: $4x - 8 = 2x + 6$. Subtract $2x$: $2x - 8 = 6$. Add $8$: $2x = 14$, so $x = 7$.

### Example 4: fractions

$$\frac{x}{2} + \frac{x}{3} = 10$$

Get rid of the fractions by multiplying both sides by the least common denominator, $6$:

$$3x + 2x = 60 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 5x = 60 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 12$$

> **Key idea:** If an equation contains fractions, multiply **every** term on both sides by the common denominator first. The fractions disappear and the rest is routine.

## Special cases

Usually a linear equation has exactly one solution. But two other outcomes are possible – you notice them when the variable disappears:

- **No solution:** $2x + 1 = 2x + 5$ leads to $1 = 5$, a false statement. No value of $x$ works.
- **Infinitely many solutions:** $2(x + 1) = 2x + 2$ leads to $2 = 2$, always true. Every number is a solution.

## Word problems

To solve a word problem, choose a variable for the unknown quantity, translate the text into an equation, solve it, and answer in words.

### Example 5

Three consecutive whole numbers add up to $48$. Which numbers are they?

Let the smallest be $x$. Then the others are $x + 1$ and $x + 2$:

$$x + (x + 1) + (x + 2) = 48 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 3x + 3 = 48 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 3x = 45 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 15$$

The numbers are $15$, $16$ and $17$.

## Rearranging formulas

The same steps work when the "numbers" are letters. To solve the formula for the area of a rectangle $A = l \cdot w$ for $w$, divide both sides by $l$: $w = \frac{A}{l}$.

## Common mistakes

- Doing an operation on only one side, or only to some of the terms.
- Moving a term across the equals sign without changing its sign: from $x + 4 = 9$ you get $x = 9 - 4$, not $x = 9 + 4$.
- When multiplying by a common denominator, forgetting to multiply terms that are not fractions.
- Sign errors when expanding brackets with a minus in front, e.g. $-(x - 3) = -x + 3$.
- Dividing by the coefficient incorrectly: from $-3x = 12$ you get $x = -4$, not $x = 4$.
- Skipping the check – it catches most of these errors.

## Practice

1. Solve $7x - 4 = 24$.

<summary>Solution</summary>

Add $4$: $7x = 28$. Divide by $7$: $x = 4$.

</details>

2. Solve $3(x + 2) = x + 16$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$3x + 6 = x + 16$. Subtract $x$ and $6$: $2x = 10$, so $x = 5$. Check: $3 \cdot 7 = 21$ and $5 + 16 = 21$.

</details>

3. Solve $6 - 2(x - 1) = 4x - 10$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Expand: $6 - 2x + 2 = 4x - 10$, so $8 - 2x = 4x - 10$. Add $2x$ and $10$: $18 = 6x$, so $x = 3$.

</details>

4. Solve $\frac{x + 1}{4} = \frac{x - 2}{3}$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply by $12$: $3(x + 1) = 4(x - 2)$, so $3x + 3 = 4x - 8$. Subtract $3x$ and add $8$: $x = 11$.

</details>

5. Anna is three times as old as Ben. In 10 years she will be twice as old as Ben. How old are they now?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Let Ben be $b$ years old, Anna $3b$. In 10 years: $3b + 10 = 2(b + 10)$, so $3b + 10 = 2b + 20$ and $b = 10$. Ben is 10, Anna is 30. Check: in 10 years they are 20 and 40.

</details>
