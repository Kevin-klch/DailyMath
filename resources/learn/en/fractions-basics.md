A fraction describes a part of a whole – or, more generally, the result of a division that does not have to come out evenly. Fractions are everywhere: half a pizza, three quarters of an hour, a probability of one in six. Once you understand what the two numbers in a fraction mean, simplifying, expanding and comparing fractions become straightforward.

## Numerator and denominator

A fraction is written with two whole numbers separated by a fraction bar:

$$\frac{3}{4}$$

- The **denominator** (bottom, here $4$) tells you into how many equal parts the whole is divided.
- The **numerator** (top, here $3$) tells you how many of those parts you take.

So $\frac{3}{4}$ means: cut a whole into $4$ equal pieces and take $3$ of them. The denominator can never be $0$ – you cannot divide something into zero parts.

### Fractions as division

The fraction bar is just another way of writing division:

$$\frac{3}{4} = 3 : 4 = 0.75$$

This also explains why every whole number is a fraction: $5 = \frac{5}{1} = \frac{10}{2}$.

## Proper and improper fractions

- A **proper fraction** has a numerator smaller than its denominator, for example $\frac{2}{5}$. Its value is less than $1$.
- An **improper fraction** has a numerator greater than or equal to its denominator, for example $\frac{7}{4}$. Its value is at least $1$. Improper fractions can also be written as mixed numbers: $\frac{7}{4} = 1\frac{3}{4}$.
- A **unit fraction** has numerator $1$, such as $\frac{1}{8}$.

## Equivalent fractions

Different fractions can describe exactly the same amount. Half a cake is the same as two quarters or four eighths:

$$\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{4}{8}$$

Such fractions are called **equivalent**. You create them by expanding or simplifying.

### Expanding

To **expand** a fraction, multiply numerator and denominator by the same number (not zero):

$$\frac{2}{3} = \frac{2 \cdot 4}{3 \cdot 4} = \frac{8}{12}$$

The pieces become smaller, but you take correspondingly more of them – the amount stays the same.

### Simplifying

To **simplify** (or reduce) a fraction, divide numerator and denominator by the same number:

$$\frac{18}{24} = \frac{18 : 6}{24 : 6} = \frac{3}{4}$$

A fraction is in **lowest terms** when numerator and denominator have no common factor other than $1$. You get there in one step by dividing by the greatest common divisor (GCD) of both numbers. If you do not see the GCD right away, you can also simplify in several smaller steps.

> **Key idea:** Multiplying or dividing numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number does not change the value of a fraction. Simplify as far as possible – fully reduced fractions are easier to compare and to work with.

## Comparing fractions

Which is larger, $\frac{3}{5}$ or $\frac{2}{3}$? There are a few strategies:

- **Same denominator:** compare the numerators. $\frac{5}{9} > \frac{4}{9}$.
- **Same numerator:** the fraction with the *smaller* denominator is larger, because its pieces are bigger. $\frac{3}{4} > \frac{3}{7}$.
- **Otherwise:** expand both to a common denominator, then compare numerators.

### Example 1

Compare $\frac{3}{5}$ and $\frac{2}{3}$.

A common denominator is $15$:

$$\frac{3}{5} = \frac{9}{15}, \qquad \frac{2}{3} = \frac{10}{15}$$

Since $9 < 10$, we get $\frac{3}{5} < \frac{2}{3}$.

### Example 2

Reduce $\frac{42}{56}$ to lowest terms.

Both numbers are divisible by $7$: $\frac{42}{56} = \frac{6}{8}$. Both are still even: $\frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}$. In one step you could divide by the GCD $14$ directly.

### Example 3

What fraction of an hour is $20$ minutes?

An hour has $60$ minutes, so it is $\frac{20}{60} = \frac{1}{3}$ of an hour.

## Fractions of an amount

To find a fraction of a quantity, divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator:

$$\frac{3}{4} \text{ of } 80 = 80 : 4 \cdot 3 = 20 \cdot 3 = 60$$

## Common mistakes

- Adding or subtracting the same number to numerator and denominator: $\frac{2}{3} \neq \frac{2+1}{3+1}$. Only multiplying or dividing keeps the value.
- Simplifying a sum term by term: in $\frac{2 + 6}{2}$ you cannot just cancel the $2$s. The correct value is $\frac{8}{2} = 4$.
- Thinking a larger denominator means a larger fraction: $\frac{1}{8}$ is smaller than $\frac{1}{4}$.
- Stopping too early when simplifying, for example leaving $\frac{6}{8}$ instead of $\frac{3}{4}$.

## Practice

1. Reduce $\frac{36}{48}$ to lowest terms.

The GCD of $36$ and $48$ is $12$: $\frac{36 : 12}{48 : 12} = \frac{3}{4}$.

2. Expand $\frac{5}{6}$ so that the denominator is $42$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$42 : 6 = 7$, so multiply both parts by $7$: $\frac{5 \cdot 7}{6 \cdot 7} = \frac{35}{42}$.

</details>

3. Which is larger: $\frac{5}{8}$ or $\frac{7}{12}$?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Common denominator $24$: $\frac{5}{8} = \frac{15}{24}$ and $\frac{7}{12} = \frac{14}{24}$. So $\frac{5}{8}$ is larger.

</details>

4. How much is $\frac{2}{5}$ of $35$ kg?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$35 : 5 = 7$, and $7 \cdot 2 = 14$. So it is $14$ kg.

</details>
