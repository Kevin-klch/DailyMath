A length of "5" means nothing until you know whether it is 5 mm or 5 km. Every measurement consists of a **number** and a **unit**, and many mistakes in word problems come from mixing units. This article covers the metric units used in science and most of the world, how to convert between them, and the special rules for area, volume and time.

## Metric prefixes

The metric system is built on powers of ten. A prefix in front of a base unit tells you the factor:

| Prefix | Symbol | Factor |
|---|---|---|
| kilo | k | $1000 = 10^3$ |
| hecto | h | $100 = 10^2$ |
| deci | d | $\frac{1}{10} = 10^{-1}$ |
| centi | c | $\frac{1}{100} = 10^{-2}$ |
| milli | m | $\frac{1}{1000} = 10^{-3}$ |

So $1 \text{ km} = 1000 \text{ m}$, $1 \text{ mg} = \frac{1}{1000} \text{ g}$, $1 \text{ cL} = \frac{1}{100} \text{ L}$.

## Length, mass and capacity

**Length** (base unit: metre)

$$1 \text{ km} = 1000 \text{ m} \qquad 1 \text{ m} = 10 \text{ dm} = 100 \text{ cm} = 1000 \text{ mm} \qquad 1 \text{ cm} = 10 \text{ mm}$$

**Mass** (base unit: gram; the kilogram is the official SI unit)

$$1 \text{ t} = 1000 \text{ kg} \qquad 1 \text{ kg} = 1000 \text{ g} \qquad 1 \text{ g} = 1000 \text{ mg}$$

**Capacity** (litre)

$$1 \text{ L} = 10 \text{ dL} = 100 \text{ cL} = 1000 \text{ mL}$$

## How to convert

> **Key idea:** Going from a **larger** unit to a **smaller** one, the number gets bigger – multiply. Going from a smaller unit to a larger one, the number gets smaller – divide.

That makes sense: a distance contains many more centimetres than metres.

### Example 1

- $3.2 \text{ km} = 3.2 \cdot 1000 \text{ m} = 3200 \text{ m}$
- $450 \text{ g} = 450 : 1000 \text{ kg} = 0.45 \text{ kg}$
- $2.5 \text{ L} = 2.5 \cdot 1000 \text{ mL} = 2500 \text{ mL}$

When several steps are needed, go step by step or use the combined factor: $1 \text{ km} = 1000 \text{ m} = 100\,000 \text{ cm}$.

### Converting with a fraction equal to 1

A reliable method for any conversion is to multiply by a fraction whose value is 1, chosen so that the unwanted unit cancels:

$$750 \text{ mm} \cdot \frac{1 \text{ m}}{1000 \text{ mm}} = 0.75 \text{ m}$$

The unit mm appears above and below the line and cancels, leaving metres.

## Area units

An area of $1 \text{ m}^2$ is a square with side $1 \text{ m} = 10 \text{ dm}$. Its area in square decimetres is $10 \cdot 10 = 100 \text{ dm}^2$. So area units do **not** use the factor 10 between neighbours, but $10^2 = 100$.

$$1 \text{ m}^2 = 100 \text{ dm}^2 = 10\,000 \text{ cm}^2 = 1\,000\,000 \text{ mm}^2$$

For land, people also use the are and the hectare:

$$1 \text{ a} = 100 \text{ m}^2 \qquad 1 \text{ ha} = 100 \text{ a} = 10\,000 \text{ m}^2 \qquad 1 \text{ km}^2 = 100 \text{ ha}$$

## Volume units

A cube with edge $1 \text{ m} = 10 \text{ dm}$ holds $10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 = 1000 \text{ dm}^3$. So volume units use the factor $10^3 = 1000$ between neighbours.

$$1 \text{ m}^3 = 1000 \text{ dm}^3 \qquad 1 \text{ dm}^3 = 1000 \text{ cm}^3 \qquad 1 \text{ cm}^3 = 1000 \text{ mm}^3$$

Volume and capacity are linked by

$$1 \text{ dm}^3 = 1 \text{ L} \qquad 1 \text{ cm}^3 = 1 \text{ mL}$$

> **Key idea:** Between neighbouring units the factor is 10 for length, 100 for area ($10^2$) and 1000 for volume ($10^3$). The exponent of the unit tells you the power.

### Example 2

Convert $3.5 \text{ m}^2$ to $\text{cm}^2$. From m to cm the length factor is 100, so the area factor is $100^2 = 10\,000$:

$$3.5 \text{ m}^2 = 3.5 \cdot 10\,000 \text{ cm}^2 = 35\,000 \text{ cm}^2$$

### Example 3

An aquarium measures $80 \text{ cm} \times 40 \text{ cm} \times 50 \text{ cm}$. How many litres does it hold?

$$V = 80 \cdot 40 \cdot 50 \text{ cm}^3 = 160\,000 \text{ cm}^3 = 160 \text{ dm}^3 = 160 \text{ L}$$

## Time

Time does not follow the decimal system:

$$1 \text{ d} = 24 \text{ h} \qquad 1 \text{ h} = 60 \text{ min} \qquad 1 \text{ min} = 60 \text{ s}$$

So $1 \text{ h} = 3600 \text{ s}$. Be careful with decimal hours: $1.5 \text{ h}$ is 1 hour and 30 minutes, not 1 hour and 50 minutes, because $0.5 \cdot 60 = 30$.

### Example 4: speed

Convert $72 \text{ km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$:

$$72 \frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} = \frac{72\,000 \text{ m}}{3600 \text{ s}} = 20 \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}$$

In general you divide km/h by $3.6$ to get m/s.

## Common mistakes

- Multiplying when you should divide (and vice versa). Ask: should the number get bigger or smaller?
- Using the factor 10 or 100 for area and volume units: $1 \text{ m}^2 = 10\,000 \text{ cm}^2$, not $100 \text{ cm}^2$.
- Treating time as decimal: $2.25 \text{ h}$ is 2 h 15 min, not 2 h 25 min.
- Calculating with mixed units, e.g. adding $2 \text{ m}$ and $30 \text{ cm}$ as $32$. Convert first: $2.3 \text{ m}$.
- Dropping the unit in the final answer.

## Practice

1. Convert: $4.7 \text{ km}$ to m, $85 \text{ mm}$ to cm, $1250 \text{ g}$ to kg.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$4.7 \text{ km} = 4700 \text{ m}$, $85 \text{ mm} = 8.5 \text{ cm}$, $1250 \text{ g} = 1.25 \text{ kg}$.

</details>

2. Convert $2.4 \text{ dm}^2$ to $\text{cm}^2$ and $50\,000 \text{ m}^2$ to hectares.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$2.4 \text{ dm}^2 = 2.4 \cdot 100 \text{ cm}^2 = 240 \text{ cm}^2$. $50\,000 \text{ m}^2 = 50\,000 : 10\,000 \text{ ha} = 5 \text{ ha}$.

</details>

3. How many litres are $0.6 \text{ m}^3$? How many $\text{cm}^3$ are $0.25 \text{ L}$?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$0.6 \text{ m}^3 = 600 \text{ dm}^3 = 600 \text{ L}$. $0.25 \text{ L} = 250 \text{ mL} = 250 \text{ cm}^3$.

</details>

4. Write $2.75 \text{ h}$ in hours and minutes, and $195 \text{ min}$ in hours.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$0.75 \cdot 60 = 45$, so $2.75 \text{ h} = 2 \text{ h } 45 \text{ min}$. $195 : 60 = 3.25$, so $195 \text{ min} = 3.25 \text{ h}$ (3 h 15 min).

</details>

5. A cyclist rides at $5 \text{ m/s}$. What is that in km/h?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

In one hour: $5 \cdot 3600 = 18\,000 \text{ m} = 18 \text{ km}$. So $5 \text{ m/s} = 18 \text{ km/h}$ (or: $5 \cdot 3.6 = 18$).

</details>
