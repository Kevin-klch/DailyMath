An **angle** is formed when two rays start at the same point. Angles describe how far something is turned – from the corner of a sheet of paper to the hands of a clock. Almost every geometry problem involves angles, so it pays to know the basic facts by heart.

## What an angle is

Picture two rays that share a starting point, the **vertex**. The two rays are the **arms** of the angle. The size of the angle tells you how far you would have to rotate one arm around the vertex to land on the other one.

Angles are usually named with Greek letters such as $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$, or with three points: $\angle ABC$ is the angle at vertex $B$ between the rays towards $A$ and towards $C$. The middle letter is always the vertex.

## Measuring in degrees

A full turn is divided into $360$ equal parts, called **degrees**:

$$\text{full turn} = 360^\circ \qquad \text{half turn} = 180^\circ \qquad \text{quarter turn} = 90^\circ$$

To measure an angle with a protractor, place its centre on the vertex, line up the zero mark with one arm and read the value where the other arm crosses the scale. Make sure you read the scale that starts at zero on your arm.

## Types of angles

| Name | Size |
|---|---|
| acute angle | between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ |
| right angle | exactly $90^\circ$ |
| obtuse angle | between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$ |
| straight angle | exactly $180^\circ$ (the arms form a straight line) |
| reflex angle | between $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$ |
| full angle | exactly $360^\circ$ |

In drawings, a right angle is marked with a small square in the corner instead of an arc. Two lines that meet at a right angle are called **perpendicular**.

## Angle pairs

Some pairs of angles have fixed relationships, and these are the tools you use most often.

- **Complementary angles** add up to $90^\circ$. The complement of $35^\circ$ is $55^\circ$.
- **Supplementary angles** add up to $180^\circ$. The supplement of $35^\circ$ is $145^\circ$.
- **Adjacent angles on a straight line**: if a ray stands on a straight line, the two angles it creates share an arm and together form a straight angle, so they are supplementary.
- **Vertical angles**: when two straight lines cross, four angles appear. The angles directly opposite each other are equal. Neighbouring angles are supplementary.

> **Key idea:** Angles on a straight line add up to $180^\circ$, angles around a point add up to $360^\circ$, and vertical angles are equal.

## Parallel lines and a transversal

Imagine two parallel horizontal lines, crossed by a third, slanted line (the **transversal**). At each of the two crossing points, four angles appear – eight angles in total. Because the lines are parallel, the picture at the upper crossing is an exact copy of the picture at the lower one. This gives three rules:

- **Corresponding angles** sit in the same position at the two crossings (for example both "above the line, right of the transversal"). They are equal.
- **Alternate angles** lie on opposite sides of the transversal and between the parallel lines, forming a "Z" shape. They are equal.
- **Co-interior angles** (also called same-side interior angles) lie on the same side of the transversal and between the parallel lines, forming a "C" or "U" shape. They add up to $180^\circ$.

> **Key idea:** With parallel lines, corresponding angles and alternate angles are equal; co-interior angles are supplementary. The converse also holds: if corresponding angles are equal, the lines are parallel.

## Angle sums in polygons

The three angles of any triangle add up to $180^\circ$. A polygon with $n$ corners can be cut into $n - 2$ triangles from one vertex, so its interior angles add up to

$$(n - 2) \cdot 180^\circ$$

A quadrilateral therefore has an angle sum of $360^\circ$, a pentagon $540^\circ$.

## Worked examples

### Example 1

Two supplementary angles are in the ratio $1 : 2$. How large are they?

Call them $x$ and $2x$. Then

$$x + 2x = 180^\circ \quad\Rightarrow\quad 3x = 180^\circ \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 60^\circ$$

The angles are $60^\circ$ and $120^\circ$.

### Example 2

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. Two alternate angles are given as $3x + 10^\circ$ and $5x - 30^\circ$. Find $x$ and the angle.

Alternate angles are equal:

$$3x + 10^\circ = 5x - 30^\circ \quad\Rightarrow\quad 40^\circ = 2x \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 20^\circ$$

The angle is $3 \cdot 20^\circ + 10^\circ = 70^\circ$. Check: $5 \cdot 20^\circ - 30^\circ = 70^\circ$.

### Example 3

Two lines cross and one of the four angles is $48^\circ$. The opposite angle is also $48^\circ$; the two other angles are each $180^\circ - 48^\circ = 132^\circ$. Check: $48^\circ + 132^\circ + 48^\circ + 132^\circ = 360^\circ$.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up complementary ($90^\circ$) and supplementary ($180^\circ$).
- Reading the wrong scale on the protractor and getting $180^\circ$ minus the real value.
- Using the parallel-line rules when the lines are *not* parallel – then corresponding angles are generally different.
- Forgetting that in $\angle ABC$ the vertex is the middle letter $B$.
- Thinking co-interior angles are equal – they add up to $180^\circ$.

## Practice

1. Find the complement and the supplement of $37^\circ$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Complement: $90^\circ - 37^\circ = 53^\circ$. Supplement: $180^\circ - 37^\circ = 143^\circ$.

</details>

2. Two lines intersect and one angle is $65^\circ$. Find the other three angles.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The vertical angle is also $65^\circ$. The neighbouring angles are $180^\circ - 65^\circ = 115^\circ$ each. So the angles are $65^\circ$, $115^\circ$, $65^\circ$, $115^\circ$.

</details>

3. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One angle is $72^\circ$. How large is the co-interior angle on the same side?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Co-interior angles are supplementary: $180^\circ - 72^\circ = 108^\circ$.

</details>

4. Two angles of a triangle measure $48^\circ$ and $67^\circ$. Find the third angle.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$180^\circ - 48^\circ - 67^\circ = 65^\circ$.

</details>

5. What is the sum of the interior angles of a hexagon? How large is each angle in a regular hexagon?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$(6 - 2) \cdot 180^\circ = 720^\circ$. In a regular hexagon all six angles are equal: $720^\circ : 6 = 120^\circ$.

</details>
