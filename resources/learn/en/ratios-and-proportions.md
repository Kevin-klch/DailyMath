A ratio compares two quantities: "for every 2 cups of flour, use 3 eggs". Ratios appear in recipes, maps, mixtures and speeds, and they lead straight to one of the most useful everyday tools in mathematics – the **rule of three**. With it you can scale a recipe, work out unit prices or figure out how long a job takes with more people.

## What a ratio is

The ratio of $a$ to $b$ is written $a : b$ and read "$a$ to $b$". It says how many times as large one quantity is compared to the other. A ratio can also be written as a fraction $\frac{a}{b}$.

Like fractions, ratios can be simplified by dividing both parts by the same number:

$$12 : 18 = 2 : 3$$

The order matters: if a class has 12 girls and 18 boys, the ratio of girls to boys is $2 : 3$, but the ratio of boys to girls is $3 : 2$.

## Sharing in a ratio

To divide an amount in the ratio $a : b$, split it into $a + b$ equal parts.

### Example 1

Two friends share 60 € in the ratio $2 : 3$.

There are $2 + 3 = 5$ parts, so one part is $60 : 5 = 12$. The first friend gets $2 \cdot 12 = 24$, the second $3 \cdot 12 = 36$, so 24 € and 36 €. Check: $24 + 36 = 60$.

## Proportions

An equation between two ratios is a **proportion**:

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$$

Multiplying both sides by $b \cdot d$ gives the **cross-multiplication** rule:

> **Key idea:** $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$ exactly when $a \cdot d = b \cdot c$ (for $b, d \neq 0$). Use this to find a missing value in a proportion.

### Example 2

Solve $\frac{3}{5} = \frac{x}{20}$.

Cross-multiply: $5x = 3 \cdot 20 = 60$, so $x = 12$.

## Direct proportion

Two quantities are **directly proportional** if doubling one doubles the other, tripling one triples the other, and so on. Their quotient stays constant. The price of apples and their weight are a typical example.

### The rule of three

The rule of three solves such problems in three steps: from the given amount, go to **one unit**, then to the amount you want.

### Example 3

$4$ notebooks cost 6 €. How much do $7$ notebooks cost?

$$4 \text{ notebooks} \;\to\; 6 \qquad \text{(divide by 4)}$$

$$1 \text{ notebook} \;\to\; 1.5 \qquad \text{(multiply by 7)}$$

$$7 \text{ notebooks} \;\to\; 10.5$$

So $7$ notebooks cost 10.50 €. Both columns are divided and multiplied by the **same** number.

## Inverse proportion

Two quantities are **inversely proportional** if doubling one *halves* the other. Their product stays constant. Example: the more workers share a job, the less time it takes (assuming they all work equally fast).

### Example 4

$6$ workers need $10$ days to build a wall. How long do $4$ workers need?

$$6 \text{ workers} \;\to\; 10 \text{ days}$$

$$1 \text{ worker} \;\to\; 60 \text{ days} \qquad \text{(fewer workers, more time: multiply)}$$

$$4 \text{ workers} \;\to\; 15 \text{ days} \qquad \text{(more workers, less time: divide)}$$

Check: $6 \cdot 10 = 60 = 4 \cdot 15$ – the product is constant.

> **Key idea:** In a direct proportion both quantities change in the same way (multiply one, multiply the other). In an inverse proportion they change in opposite ways (multiply one, divide the other). Always ask first: "more of this means more or less of that?"

## Scale

A map scale is a ratio. On a map with scale $1 : 25000$, $1$ cm on the map stands for $25000$ cm in reality. So $4$ cm on the map correspond to $4 \cdot 25000 = 100000$ cm, which is $1$ km.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up the order of a ratio: $2 : 3$ is not the same as $3 : 2$.
- Sharing in the ratio $2 : 3$ by halves instead of fifths.
- Using direct proportion where the relationship is inverse (or not proportional at all – a person's height is not proportional to their age).
- In the rule of three, doing different operations on the two columns.

## Practice

1. Simplify the ratio $45 : 60$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Divide both parts by $15$: $45 : 60 = 3 : 4$.

</details>

2. Share 84 € in the ratio $3 : 4$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$3 + 4 = 7$ parts, one part is $84 : 7 = 12$. The shares are $3 \cdot 12 = 36$ and $4 \cdot 12 = 48$, so 36 € and 48 €.

</details>

3. $5$ kg of apples cost 12 €. How much do $8$ kg cost?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$1$ kg costs $12 : 5 = 2.4$, so 2.40 €. Then $8$ kg cost $8 \cdot 2.4 = 19.2$, i.e. 19.20 €.

</details>

4. $3$ pumps empty a pool in $8$ hours. How long do $4$ pumps take?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Inverse proportion: $1$ pump needs $3 \cdot 8 = 24$ hours, so $4$ pumps need $24 : 4 = 6$ hours.

</details>

5. Solve $\frac{x}{12} = \frac{5}{4}$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Cross-multiply: $4x = 5 \cdot 12 = 60$, so $x = 15$.

</details>
